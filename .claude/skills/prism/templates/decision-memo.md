# Template — Decision Memo

For when the audience must approve, fund, sponsor, prioritise, or choose. The default for decision support. Put the ask first; a decision memo that buries the ask has failed.

## Instructions
- The decision requested goes in the first two lines. No throat-clearing.
- Give options with trade-offs, then a clear recommendation — never options without a pick.
- Pre-empt the objection that actually blocks the yes.
- One page where possible. Quantify. Name the owner and timing of the ask.
- For an **escalation memo**, use the same shape but open with the risk and its impact.

## Structure

```
# Decision Memo — <subject>
**To:** <decider(s)>  **From:** <you/team>  **Date:** <date>  **Decision needed by:** <date>

## Decision requested
<the specific yes/no or choice, the size, the owner. One or two sentences.>

## Why now
<the trigger and the cost of delay — make inaction expensive and concrete>

## Context
<the minimum situation needed to decide — 3–5 lines, no history dump>

## Options
| Option | What it involves | Pros | Cons / risk | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (recommended) | … | … | … | … |
| B | … | … | … | … |
| C — do nothing | … | … | <cost of inaction> | … |

## Recommendation
<Option A, and the one-line reason it wins>

## Risks & mitigations
<top risks of the recommended option, each with a mitigation>

## Objections pre-empted
<the 2–3 real objections → your response to each>

## The ask
<exactly what you need the decider to do, by when, and what happens next once they say yes>
```
