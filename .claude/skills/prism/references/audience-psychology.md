# Audience Psychology

PRISM treats communication as **audience state change**. You are not delivering information; you are moving specific people from a current state to a desired state. This file is how to model that state (workflow steps 7–13).

## Model the audience along these dimensions

- **Beliefs** — what they currently hold as true about the topic, the team, the risk. Some will be wrong; some will be load-bearing for their identity or past decisions.
- **Concerns / fears** — what they are worried about (being blamed, wasting money, looking uninformed, losing control, disruption to their function).
- **Incentives** — what they are measured and rewarded on. People act toward their incentives, not your logic. A CFO's cost lens and a CPO's growth lens read the same slide differently.
- **Trust** — do they trust the messenger, the source of evidence, the team proposing this? Low trust means even strong evidence gets discounted.
- **Risk perception** — how they weigh the risk of acting vs the risk of not acting. Most audiences over-weight the risk of change and under-weight the risk of inaction; making the cost of inaction concrete is often the whole job.
- **Objection patterns** — the predictable "yes, but…": too expensive, too risky, wrong time, not our priority, unproven, who owns it, we tried this before.
- **Decision-making constraints** — how decisions actually get made here: who really decides, what a yes requires, committee vs individual, what a "no decision" looks like (usually: silent deferral).
- **Confidence thresholds** — how much certainty they need before they'll commit. Understanding is cheap; confidence-to-act is expensive. Name what would get them over the line.
- **Attention constraints** — how much time and cognitive load they'll give this. Assume less than you'd like. The recommendation must survive a 60-second skim.
- **Status-quo bias** — the default is to do nothing; doing nothing feels safe and requires no defence. You are always competing against inertia, not just against alternatives.

## The three-part frame (fill this for every request)

1. **What must they STOP believing?** (the belief blocking the outcome)
2. **What must they START believing?** (the belief enabling it)
3. **What must they feel confident enough to DO?** (the action, and the confidence it requires)

## Do not collapse the goal into "persuasion"

Name precisely what state change you need — they are different, and they need different artifacts and evidence:

- **Understanding** — they can accurately describe the situation. (education)
- **Agreement** — they accept your framing/conclusion. (alignment / persuasion)
- **Confidence** — they believe it enough to bet on it. (evidence, risk mitigation)
- **Urgency** — they believe it must happen *now*, not later. (cost of inaction)
- **Trust** — they believe *you/the source*. (credibility, track record, references)
- **Alignment** — the group holds a shared view. (workshop, shared language)
- **Decision** — a specific yes/no is made. (decision memo)
- **Action** — behaviour actually changes. (enablement, ownership, follow-through)

A common failure: producing a beautiful artifact that creates *understanding* when the outcome needed *decision* — or that seeks *agreement* when the real blocker was *trust*.

## Diagnostic questions

Ask these to locate the audience state (a subset becomes your clarification questions):

- Who literally receives this, and who actually decides?
- What do they believe about this today — and what of that is wrong or outdated?
- What are they afraid of if they say yes? If they say no?
- What are they measured on this quarter? How does this help or threaten that?
- Do they trust the team proposing this? Has a similar effort failed before?
- What evidence would they personally find credible — and what would they wave away?
- What has to be true for them to say yes? What does a "no decision" look like here?
- How much attention will they realistically give this? Read or presented? How long?
- What is the cost of them doing nothing — and do they currently feel it?
